# u9-hotstove | McGregor's Hot Stove rules | Controlling › McGregor's "Hot Stove" Rules
> Douglas McGregor (1967) gave four rules for fair and constructive discipline. 1 Forewarning: employees know the rules and consequences beforehand (no surprises, like knowing the stove is hot). 2 Immediate consequences: discipline right after the rule is broken (like the instant burn). 3 Consistency: every time the rule is broken, the same consequence follows (rules are firm and predictable). 4 Impartiality: everyone who breaks the rule gets the same consequence regardless of position (fairness and morale).
~ "FICI": Forewarning, Immediate, Consistency, Impartiality. Warned, Now, Always, Everyone.

MS2 | A nurse gives the wrong medication because two drugs have very similar packaging. She reports it immediately. Under Just Culture, what is this and what is the right response?
+ Human error; improve the system (e.g., change storage or packaging) :: It was unintentional. The module says system improvements prevent recurrence.
- Reckless behavior; immediate corrective action :: She did not knowingly ignore safety.
- At-risk behavior; coaching :: She made no risky choice. The packaging caused the slip.
- No action is needed because it was reported :: The system should still be improved.

MS2 | A nurse skips checking the wristband because "I've cared for him for three days, I know him." Under Just Culture, what is this and the right response?
+ At-risk behavior; coaching to reinforce protocols :: Skipping a check by choice raises risk but is not meant to harm. This is the module's at-risk example.
- Human error; system redesign :: This was a choice, not an accidental slip.
- Reckless behavior; termination :: Reckless behavior knowingly disregards safety protocols with conscious risk. The module classifies this example as at-risk.
- No action because no harm occurred :: At-risk behavior still needs coaching.

MS2 | A doctor in a rush knowingly skips sterile procedure during a central line insertion, risking infection. Under Just Culture, what is this?
+ Reckless behavior that needs immediate corrective action :: Knowingly disregarding safety protocols is reckless. This is the module's example.
- Human error that needs system improvement :: It was a conscious choice, not an accident.
- At-risk behavior that needs coaching only :: Knowingly risking harm goes beyond at-risk behavior.
- A near miss :: The risk was knowingly taken during an actual procedure.

M3 | What is the goal of a just culture according to the module?
+ Staff feel safe to report errors and near misses without unjust punishment, while reckless behavior is still held accountable :: Just culture balances learning with accountability.
- Nobody is ever held accountable for anything :: Reckless behavior is still held accountable.
- Every error leads to suspension :: That is a blame culture.
- Only mandatory reports are accepted :: Voluntary reporting is also needed.
