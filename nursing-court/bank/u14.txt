# u14-resume | CV vs resume and resume writing | Personal and Professional Responsibilities › Resume Format
> CV = a detailed document of one's entire career, education, achievements, and experiences. Resume = a concise document summarizing skills, work experience, and qualifications TAILORED to a specific job. A resume should be professional (neat, organized, error-free), quickly capture attention (concise, highlights the most relevant), and tailored to the role. 9 key components: contact information (full name, phone, professional email, LinkedIn); objective or summary statement; education; work experience (bullet points); skills and competencies (including software like EHR); certifications and licensure (ACLS, PALS, specialty); awards and recognitions; professional development (workshops, conferences, CE); references (optional; or "References available upon request"). Guidelines: length 1 to 2 pages; clarity (direct, no jargon, bullets). Formatting: professional readable font (Arial, Times New Roman) 12-point or larger; single font style, clean layout; high-quality white or off-white paper; black with a single accent color. Exclude: marital status, age, children, ethnicity, religious affiliation; exaggerated claims. Proofread grammar and punctuation. References: only people who know you well (ideally 3+ years); inform them in advance; make sure they agree to be contacted.

= CV | curriculum vitae
:: A CV is detailed and covers the entire career.

I1 | Concise document summarizing skills, work experience, and qualifications tailored to a specific job.
= Resume | résumé
:: A resume is short and tailored.

# u14-fields | Fields of specialization in nursing | Personal and Professional Responsibilities › Fields of Specialization in Nursing
> APRNs (Advanced Practice Registered Nurses) give primary and specialized care in collaboration: NPs (nurse practitioners: diagnose, treat, prescribe, do procedures; specialize in pediatrics, geriatrics, oncology), CNSs (clinical nurse specialists: experts in an area like oncology, pediatrics, critical care; improve care, educate staff, apply evidence-based practice), CRNAs (nurse anesthetists: anesthesia for surgery), CNMs (nurse midwives: prenatal, labor, postpartum care, deliveries, family planning). Nurse educators: nursing faculty (teach undergraduate or graduate) and clinical educators (teach staff best practices in healthcare settings). Nurse administrators: nurse managers (day-to-day unit operations, staffing, compliance) and nurse executives such as CNOs (hospital- or system-wide policies). Clinical research nurses (conduct research studies with pharma, universities, institutions). Nurse scientists (advance knowledge through research). Forensic nursing (care for crime victims, collect evidence, work with law enforcement). Public health nursing (community health promotion, disease prevention, public health crises). Palliative care nursing (comfort and support for serious, life-limiting illness). Telehealth nursing (BPO: answer health inquiries, triage, basic advice, schedule appointments). Health insurance and case management (help patients with benefits and claims). Medical coding (ICD-10 = International Classification of Diseases; CPT = Current Procedural Terminology; accurate claims for reimbursement). Medical transcription (transcribe audio records; needs medical terminology and accuracy). Livescribe nursing (emerging BPO role: document patient encounters, histories, and treatment plans during live consultations).
