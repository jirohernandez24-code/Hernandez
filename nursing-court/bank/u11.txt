# u11-ethics | Ethics | Ethico-Moral Responsibility (definition)
> Ethics = the systematic study of what a person's conduct and action SHOULD be with regard to self, other human beings, and the environment; the justification of what is right or good, and the study of what a person's life and relationships should be, not necessarily what they are. Respect for people is the most basic and universal ethical principle.

# u11-principles | Ethical principles | Ethico-Moral Responsibility › Ethical Principles
> 1 Autonomy: self-determination and freedom of choice, accepting responsibility for one's decisions; supported by the legal right of self-determination (nurse supports the patient's informed choice even if he declines the recommended treatment). 2 Beneficence: actions to PROMOTE GOOD (advocating pain relief after surgery). 3 Nonmaleficence: actions to AVOID HARM (double-checking dose and history before giving meds). 4 Utility: the good of the many outweighs the wants or needs of the individual (prioritizing vaccination of high-risk groups in an outbreak). 5 Justice: fairness; treat equals equally and give fair treatment according to individual differences (equitable care regardless of status; advocating access for marginalized groups). 6 Veracity: obligation to tell the truth; honesty and transparency (telling the patient about side effects). 7 Fidelity: commitment to fulfilling promises (following protocols; keeping follow-up promises). 8 Confidentiality: keep privileged information private (secure records, share only with authorized personnel).
~ "A Big Nurse Uses Just Very Fine Care": Autonomy, Beneficence, Nonmaleficence, Utility, Justice, Veracity, Fidelity, Confidentiality

# u11-code | Code of Ethics for Filipino Nurses (BON Res. 220, s. 2004) | Ethico-Moral Responsibility › Code of Ethics
> BON Resolution No. 220, Series of 2004 promulgated the Code of Ethics for Registered Nurses. The 2004 Code guides Filipino nurses in competent, compassionate, ethical care and has 7 articles. I Preamble: health is a basic human right; nurses promote health and prevent illness, alleviate suffering, and support a peaceful death. II RNs and People: respect values, customs, and spiritual beliefs; confidentiality; empower informed decisions. III RNs and Practice: sanctity of human life; safe, effective, compassionate care; advocacy; accurate and truthful documentation. IV RNs and Co-workers: collaboration, respect, communication, integrity, honesty. V RNs, Society, and Environment: social justice, health equity, community health, environmental health and sustainability. VI RNs and the Profession: uphold integrity and standards; lifelong learning; credibility and public trust. VII Administrative Penalties, Repealing Clause, and Effectivity: violations may lead to suspension or revocation by the BON; the repealing clause annuls previous codes.

# u11-frameworks | Ethical frameworks | Ethico-Moral Responsibility › Ethical Frameworks
> Frameworks do not give direct solutions; they help clarify the values and beliefs behind decisions. 1 Utilitarianism (teleological or consequentialist): focuses on OUTCOMES; the greatest good for the greatest number; the end justifies the means (fund local workshops for many staff instead of a national conference for a few). 2 Deontological theory (from Immanuel Kant): rightness or wrongness of actions regardless of consequences; some actions are inherently right or wrong; duty-based reasoning (actions obligatory because they fulfill a duty) and rights-based reasoning (people have rights or "just due" that must be respected) (administrator refuses a cost-cutting step that violates employee rights). 3 Intuitionism: individual judgment; intuition weighs goals, duties, and rights case by case; criticized as subjective and biased. 4 Ethical relativism: decisions align with the decision-maker's cultural values and personal beliefs. Ethical universalism: principles are absolute and apply regardless of personal or cultural differences.

I2 | Ethical framework focusing on outcomes: the greatest good for the greatest number. Also called consequentialist theory.
= Utilitarianism | utilitarian | teleological theory | consequentialist theory | consequentialism
:: Utilitarianism judges actions by their results.

I2 | Ethical framework that judges actions as right or wrong regardless of their consequences, rooted in Kant's philosophy.
= Deontological theory | deontology | deontological
:: Deontology is duty- and rights-based. Some acts are right or wrong in themselves.

I2 | Philosopher whose ideas are the root of deontological theory.
= Immanuel Kant | Kant | Emanuel Kant
:: Deontological theory is rooted in Kant's philosophy.
! Source note: the module spells the name "Emanuel Kant". The usual spelling is "Immanuel Kant". Both are accepted here.

I2 | Ethical framework where the decision-maker uses intuition to weigh goals, duties, and rights in each unique case.
= Intuitionism | intuition
:: Intuitionism treats each case as unique. Critics say it can be subjective.

I2 | Framework saying ethical decisions should align with the decision-maker's cultural values and personal beliefs.
= Ethical relativism | relativism
:: Relativism = right and wrong depend on culture and personal beliefs.

I2 | Framework holding that ethical principles are absolute and apply regardless of personal or cultural differences.
= Ethical universalism | universalism
:: Universalism = the same rules apply to everyone, everywhere.
