# u8-def | Conflict and conflict management | Conflict Management (definitions)
> Conflict = internal discord from differences in ideas, values, or feelings of two or more people. Conflict management = the process by which a leader or manager identifies, addresses, and appropriately handles conflict to keep an optimal balance that promotes growth, productivity, and creativity.

I1 | Internal discord that results from differences in ideas, values, or feelings of two or more people.
= Conflict
:: The module defines conflict as internal discord caused by differences in ideas, values, or feelings between two or more people.

I1 | The process by which a leader or manager identifies, addresses, and appropriately handles conflict to keep an optimal balance that promotes growth, productivity, and creativity.
= Conflict management | conflict managing
:: Conflict management is not about removing all conflict. It keeps conflict at a balance that still helps growth, productivity, and creativity.

M2 | According to the module, what is the goal of conflict management?
+ Keep an optimal balance of conflict that promotes growth, productivity, and creativity :: The definition says "optimal balance". Some conflict is healthy because it can drive growth and creativity.
- Remove every source of conflict from the organization :: The module never says to remove all conflict. It says to keep an optimal balance.
- Decide which party is right so the manager can punish the other :: Managing conflict is about handling it well, not choosing a side to punish.
- Report all conflicts to the human resource office :: Reporting is not the goal. The leader identifies, addresses, and handles the conflict.

MS2 | Two staff nurses disagree about how to divide patient loads. The head nurse notices the disagreement early, talks to both, and keeps the unit productive. Which process is the head nurse doing?
+ Conflict management :: She identifies, addresses, and handles the conflict to keep the unit productive. That is conflict management.
- Controlling :: Controlling compares performance with standards and corrects deviations. It is not about handling disagreements.
- Supervision :: Supervision is overseeing others' work and checking progress. The task here is resolving a disagreement.
- Collective bargaining :: Collective bargaining is negotiation between employees (through a union) and employers.

I1 | Type of conflict that occurs within an individual, involving an internal struggle to clarify contradictory values or desires.

# u8-process | The conflict process (5 stages) | Conflict Management › The Conflict Process
> 1 Latent: conditions are ripe because of antecedent factors; conflict has not happened yet (hospital is short staffed). 2 Perceived: recognized logically and impersonally as an issue of rules or resources; not yet emotional; may be solved here by open communication and mutual support (relative notices delays). 3 Felt (affective): emotional, with hostility, fear, mistrust, or anger (relative believes care is neglected). 4 Manifest (overt): action is taken: withdrawing, competing, debating, or resolving; fear of retaliation, ridicule, alienation, or past experiences can stop people from acting; socialization and gender roles affect responses (relative raises voice; head nurse listens and reviews the care plan). 5 Aftermath: positive or negative outcome; poorly managed conflict re-emerges.

# u8-winwin | Goal of conflict resolution | Conflict Management › Conflict Resolution Strategies
> The optimal goal is a win-win solution for everyone. It is not always possible. Often the manager's goal is to manage the conflict in a way that lessens the perceptual differences between the parties.

I1 | The optimal outcome when resolving conflict, where all parties involved benefit.
= Win-win | win win | win-win solution
:: The module says the optimal goal is a win-win solution for all involved.

# u8-strategies | Conflict resolution strategies | Conflict Management › Conflict Resolution Strategies
> Compromising: each party gives up something; best when each gives up something of EQUAL value, otherwise it can become lose-lose (shift swap). Competing: one party pursues its goals at others' expense = win-lose; used when quick decisions are needed or one party has more knowledge (manager imposes policy without consulting). Cooperating/Accommodating: one party sacrifices its needs so the other wins; politically useful but leaves resentment (nurse takes extra patients hoping for a future favor). Smoothing: pacifies the other party and focuses on agreements over differences; reduces emotion but may not resolve the issue. Avoiding: ignoring the conflict; fine for trivial issues or when cost of resolution exceeds benefit; the issue may fester (nurse ignores colleague's behavior, morale drops). Collaborating: assertive AND cooperative, win-win; needs mutual respect and open communication (nurse and supervisor build a schedule together).

I1 | Conflict resolution strategy where each party gives up something to reach a mutually acceptable solution.
= Compromising | compromise
:: In compromising, both sides give something up.

I2 | In compromising, to avoid a lose-lose situation, both parties should give up something of this.
= Equal value | something of equal value | equal
:: If one side feels it gave up more, compromising can turn lose-lose. Equal value keeps it fair.

MS1 | Two nurses disagree on their shift schedules. They agree to swap shifts for next week, so both get time off and the unit stays fully staffed. Which strategy did they use?
+ Compromising :: Each gave up something (a shift) of equal value to reach a mutually acceptable solution. This is the module's compromising example.
- Collaborating :: Collaborating means building a new shared solution together toward a common goal. Here they simply traded equal things.
- Accommodating :: In accommodating only one party sacrifices. Here both gave something.
- Avoiding :: They did not ignore the problem. They solved it.

M2 | Compromising can be seen as optimal. When can it become a lose-lose situation?
+ When one party feels they sacrificed more than the other :: The module warns that compromising becomes lose-lose if one party feels they gave up more.
- When both parties give up something of equal value :: Equal value is exactly how to avoid lose-lose.
- When a quick decision is needed :: Quick decisions are linked to competing.
- When the issue is trivial :: Trivial issues are linked to avoiding.

I1 | Conflict resolution strategy where one party pursues its goals at the expense of others, resulting in a win-lose situation.
= Competing | competition | compete
:: Competing has one winner and one loser.

M2 | According to the module, when is competing often used?
+ When quick decisions are needed or one party has more knowledge about the situation :: The module gives these two uses of competing.
- When the cost of resolution is greater than the benefit :: That is when avoiding may be suitable.
- When both parties want to build a shared solution :: That describes collaborating.
- When one party wants a future favor :: That describes accommodating.

# u8-stress | Work-related stress: bullying, incivility, mobbing, workplace violence | Conflict Management › Work-related Stress
> Bullying: REPEATED, health-harming mistreatment of one or more targets by one or more perpetrators; abusive, threatening, humiliating, or intimidating; a pattern, not a one-time incident. Incivility: rude or disrespectful behavior on a continuum from subtle acts (eye rolling, sarcastic comments) to overt intimidation or violence. Mobbing: a GROUP of employees collectively targets one individual with harassment, bullying, or isolation (excluding, spreading rumors). Workplace violence: behaviors that cause physical harm or psychological distress; includes physical violence plus bullying, incivility, and mobbing (family member threatens a nurse).
